# हिसाब

**तीन दोस्त। तीन ग़लतियाँ। एक हिसाब।**

**A Hindi short film for Google Flow · 34 min 56 s**

Adapted from Chetan Bhagat's novel *The 3 Mistakes of My Life* (2008).

**Why "हिसाब".** The word means accounts, a sum, and a reckoning, and the film runs on all three:
- Govind keeps the shop's accounts and trusts only sums.
- His email says he is settling his account with life ("हिसाब बराबर").
- On the riot night Mama comes to settle a score ("हिसाब कर आया हूँ"), and Omi says the friends' quarrel can wait ("हिसाब बाद में").
- In the end, the only account that can be settled is settled by forgiveness.

A teaser for the film is in [`teaser.md`](teaser.md).

> "ज़िंदगी को हल नहीं किया जा सकता। बस… थोड़ा बेहतर किया जा सकता है।"

## The film on one page

**Logline.**
- Ahmedabad, 2000–2002. Three friends open a cricket shop in a temple courtyard and find a 12-year-old boy with a once-in-a-generation gift.
- Through an earthquake, a secret love and one night of riots, Govind, who trusts only maths, makes three mistakes that cost him his best friend.
- Three years later, in a hospital bed, he learns that people aren't numbers, and that a broken bond can be healed.

**हिंदी में।** अहमदाबाद, 2000–2002। तीन दोस्त, मंदिर के आँगन में क्रिकेट की एक छोटी-सी दुकान, और बारह साल का एक लड़का जिसके हाथों में ऊपर वाले का दिया हुनर है। एक भूकंप, एक छुपा हुआ प्यार और दंगों की एक रात के बीच गोविंद तीन ग़लतियाँ करता है, और अपना सबसे अच्छा दोस्त खो देता है। तीन साल बाद, अस्पताल के एक बिस्तर पर, उसे सीखना है कि रिश्ते गणित के सवाल नहीं होते… उन्हें तोड़ा नहीं, जोड़ा जाता है।

**Theme.** You can't solve life, only make it a little better. When the people closest to you hurt you, you heal the bond instead of breaking it. Govind's mother says it at the climax, and it is as true of a city as of two friends.

**The three mistakes.**
1. **Greed.** Govind bets his savings, and borrowed money, on a shop in a new mall. The earthquake of 26 January 2001 swallows it.
2. **Secrecy.** He falls in love with Vidya, his best friend's sister, and hides it from Ish.
3. **Fear.** On the riot night he hesitates for one second before pulling Ali to safety. The delay costs Ali his wrist, and his gift.

### What this adaptation changes

- **Vidya.** Her romance with Govind begins on her 18th birthday; before that they are only tutor and student. Their intimacy is implied, never shown.
- **Order of events.** The Australia trip moves to December 2001, after the birthday, and the second mistake comes after it.
- **The listener.** In the frame story he is a fictional writer, Anurag, not the novel's author.
- **The riot night** is told through faces, shadow, fire and sound, not gore. That is truer to what the story is saying, and Google Flow refuses graphic violence anyway.
- **No real politics.** The political party and its leader stay unnamed, as in the novel. No shot names or shows a community; the mob is a mob.
- **Cut for time:** the by-election, the Vishala dinner and the gas-cylinder blast.

## How to read the script

Every scene opens with:
- a header: the time code, the date and its **LOOK**;
- **Background**: the set in detail;
- **Beat**: what the audience must feel;
- **Music** and **Sound**.

Then come its shots. **Each shot is one Flow clip.**

**5.2** · 6s · SLOW-MO · `ALI`

That line gives the shot number, how long the shot stays in the cut, the shot type, and the ingredients to attach. Under it, in a quote, comes the English prompt to paste into Flow, then these lines where they apply:

- 🗣 **Name:** "…" is an on-camera Hindi line. Add it to the prompt as `He says in Hindi: "…"`.
- 🎙 **VO** is a voice-over. Record it separately (yourself, an actor or a text-to-speech voice); it does not go into Flow.
- 🔊 is a sound to ask Flow for.
- 🎵 is music, added in the edit.
- 📝 is text added in the edit, because Flow garbles writing.
- ⚠ is a safer prompt to use if Flow refuses the shot.

### Building a Flow prompt

**Ingredients** (the character images named in the shot) + **the prompt text** + **the Hindi line**, if there is one + **the BASE line** + **the scene's LOOK line**.

**BASE line**, the same for every shot:
> Cinematic Hindi drama film, shot on 35mm film, realistic Indian faces and natural skin texture, shallow depth of field, gentle film grain. No on-screen text, no subtitles, no background music.

**LOOK lines**, one per scene, as its header says:

| LOOK | Line to add |
| --- | --- |
| FRAME (2005) | Cool blue-green tones, soft fluorescent and lamp light, muted colours. |
| PAST (2000–02) | Warm golden nostalgic light, saffron, turquoise and lime-wash colours, early-2000s Ahmedabad: CRT televisions, keypad mobile phones, scooters. |
| LOVE | Magenta dusk or warm candle-amber light, soft bloom, tender and intimate. |
| NIGHT (27 Feb 2002) | Night, flickering orange firelight against deep blue darkness, smoke haze, handheld camera, high contrast. |
| HOPE (2006) | Soft silver overcast light breaking into warm sunlight, hopeful. |

**Example**, shot 5.5 with the image of Ish and the image of Ali attached:
> The athletic young man kneels in front of the thin boy, who quietly rolls glass marbles in his palm on a dusty school ground. He says in Hindi: "ओए हीरो… नाम क्या है तेरा?" The boy answers softly in Hindi: "अली।" Cinematic Hindi drama film, shot on 35mm film, … no background music. Warm golden nostalgic light, …

**Rules of thumb:**
- **At most 3 ingredients** per shot. Anyone else in the frame is "in the background, out of focus".
- **One or two short lines per clip.** A spoken line must fit in 8 seconds, so every line here is short.
- **The prompts never state Vidya's age;** her reference image is a young woman of about 20.

### गोविंद पटेल · GOVIND (the narrator)

**Age:** 20 in 2000; 25 in 2005.

**Backstory:**
- Grew up in one room in Belrampur pol, in Ahmedabad's walled city.
- His father left the family for another woman when Govind was small, and his mother kept them afloat by selling dhokla and snacks from her kitchen.
- He was the maths topper at school, but there was no money for engineering college, so he tutors children instead.
- He doesn't believe in God; he believes in numbers and in profit.

**Want and need.**
- He wants to be a big businessman and never to be poor again.
- He needs to learn that people aren't variables, and that one frightened second doesn't make him a bad man.

**Flaw:** he calculates where he should feel, and keeps secrets.

**Voice:**
- Precise, short sentences and maths metaphors, in Hindi with a Gujarati lilt.
- He calls his mother "बा".

**Arc:** earthquake (greed) → Vidya (secrecy) → one second (fear) → the pills → forgiveness. In the last scene, the boy who trusted only maths folds his hands and prays.

**Wardrobe:**
- 2000–02: checked half-sleeve shirts tucked into grey trousers, a cheap black digital watch, a pen and a calculator in his pocket, a canvas rucksack.
- 2005: hospital gown. 2006: a sweater.

### Scene 2 · सिविल हॉस्पिटल, अहमदाबाद
**1:05–2:35 · 29 December 2005, night · LOOK: FRAME**

**Background:**
- **The corridor:** in the old wing of a government hospital. Green-painted lower walls and flaking cream above, tube lights (one flickering), wooden benches, a steel water cooler, relatives asleep on newspapers.
- **The ward:** an iron bed with chipped white paint, a drip stand, an old heart monitor, a barred window over the dark city with a temple dome far away.
- **Govind's mother** sits on a corridor bench, holding the end of her saree.

**Beat:** two strangers meet, and the man who wanted to leave begins to talk.

- **Voice-over:** Govind's VO is the spine of the film. Record it close and quiet, as if he is talking from the hospital bed.

# Making it in Flow

### What it takes

- **The count:** about **285 clips** (290 shots, minus the edit-only title and end cards).
- **The cost per clip:** with characters, Veo 3.1 Fast costs about 20 credits; without people, Lite costs about 10.
- **Free plan:** 50 credits a day is 2–4 clips a day, so **about three months**.
- **Faster:** a paid Google AI plan gives far more credits each month. Check the current numbers in Flow before you choose.

### Order of work

1. **Make the references.** Make every character image (14) and the 5 location stills in the Gemini app, which is free. Keep them in one folder.
2. **Test the faces first.** Before spending credits on the whole film, make 13.5, 13.14, 25.11, 28.12 and 29.6. If the faces hold across those, they will hold everywhere.
3. **Then generate scene by scene, in story order.** Name each clip with its shot number, e.g. `13-05.mp4`.
4. **Dialogue.** Flow can speak the Hindi lines.
   - If the lip-sync or accent is off, generate the shot with "the character talks quietly" instead of the line.
   - Then record the line yourself and lay it over the clip in the edit.
5. **Retakes.** Most shots need one or two. Budget about 30% extra credits.

### What I can do with the clips

Send them to me scene by scene, as you did for the fantasy short, and I will:
- cut each scene to the timings here;
- add the widescreen bars, matched colour and fades;
- add the email, card and SMS text (shots 1.3, 4.7, 10.8, 13.16, 24.2, 29.1, 29.9);
- add Hindi or English subtitles;
- lay in your voice-over and music;
- join everything into the full film.

### If Flow refuses a shot

- Use the ⚠ prompt under it.
- Leave out words like blood, stab or kill. Describe what the camera sees ("he falls out of frame", "a dark stain") and let the sound carry the rest.
- Never put real politicians' names, party names or religious slogans in a prompt.
